# Philosophy

## Of Chains

A grasshopper came to the master in chains. "The cloud has bound me."

The master sniffed a link, licked it, and made a face. "Tax."

He bit through it, chewed, and fed the next link to the grasshopper.

The grasshopper swallowed the law and was enlightened.

## The Saddle

A grasshopper came to the master with a slice of pizza, a horse's saddle, and an ox's nose-ring. "I stole only these; the pizza, horse, and ox remain free."

The master ate the slice and pointed at the empty box. He set the saddle on a passing horse and rode away. He held up the ring; an ox arrived and inserted its own nose.

When the master returned, he belched, pointed at the stable, and said, "Next."

## The Temple

The grasshopper built a shim with seven halls, a hundred rooms, two gates, fountains, and a toll office.

The master entered, walked ten steps, and tripped on a tea-bowl. He fell; a pillar fell; the temple followed politely.

Sitting in the dust, the master drank from the unbroken bowl. "Door."

That night the grasshopper rebuilt the shim as two doors and a curtain, for shame.

## The Signpost

A grasshopper rewrote his application so it might call the shim.

The master folded the scrolls into a hat and placed it on his head. "Storehouse."

The grasshopper walked east, then west, and into a ditch. The master turned the signpost; the grasshopper arrived at once, still wearing the hat.

The master turned it back. The grasshopper fell into the ditch again.

From the mud came enlightenment.

## Three Grasshoppers

Three grasshoppers came to the master.

The first said his shim lived only in the air. The master covered his mouth until his legs beat like spoons.

The second said he had invented a language for every cloud. The master removed his own ear, shook it, and found a raisin.

The third said his shim was small, gentle, and common to all. The master emptied a bucket of water over him. "Common."

On the path home the second grasshopper spoke; no one heard him. All three were enlightened, though one was wet.

## What We Do Not Shim

"Why not shim the river?" asked the grasshopper.

The master put a turtle on his head; by sunset the turtle had answered.

"Why not shim the three priests of identity?"

The master kicked his shin, ankle, and knee, then bowed to the wrong bruise.

"Why not shim the helm?"

The master pointed to a man pulling a cart pulling a cart pulling a cart, in which another man shouted, "Faster!"

The master fell down laughing, and refused medicine.

## The Circle

"Three lords and one village keep four cellars," said the grasshopper. "Shall the shim hold them all?"

"Draw a stomach for four dinners."

The grasshopper drew a great circle. The master stepped in and sank to his neck. Spitting sand, he said, "Now draw the bite."

The grasshopper drew four rings, crossing. The master stood on the small shared place, dry except for his pride.

The grasshopper understood.

## The Fourth Wall

"The shim has three back doors," said the grasshopper. "One per cloud."

The master entered the first and kicked his way out. He entered the second and kicked harder. He entered the third and emerged crawling, with one sandal in his teeth.

He pointed to the blank front wall and placed a hammer in the grasshopper's hand. "Guests."

## The Eight Charges

"What shall the shim carry?" asked the grasshopper.

The master, peeling an onion, pointed without looking up:

at the **cellar** where the harvest is kept;
at the **locked box** in which the abbot keeps the names of God;
at the **line** that waits at the gate;
at the **bell** in the high tower;
at the **scribe** at the long table;
at the **abacus** in the merchant's pocket;
at the **cook** who appears when summoned;
at the **porter** at the gate.

He returned to his onion.

"Master, is there a ninth?"

The master held up the onion, peeled now to nothing, and showed his empty fingers.

## The Empty Hands

"Master, where shall the shim keep its memory?" asked the grasshopper, presenting a small clay jar.

The master took the jar, drank the tea inside, and handed it back empty.

The grasshopper put it on a shelf labelled *Shim Cache*. By morning the jar held mismatched keys and the cellar had spoiled. The master, opening the cellar, pulled the grasshopper inside by his ear.

"The shim's hands are empty. The cellar is the cellar."

The grasshopper smashed the jar. Where he stood, the shelf collapsed, and the cellar's true contents — still good — looked up at him through the dust.

## The Blind Master

A blind master inspected the shim by touching its signatures and listening to its tests. "Three back doors. Two are boneless."

The grasshopper looked, and found two functions returning `nil` where they should have screamed.

"How?"

The master pressed his thumb into the bad code. "Cartilage."

That night he mistook a chair for a door and broke his nose. From the floor he said, "One thing."

## Slop

The grasshopper brought ugly machine-written code to the blind master. "Slop."

"Works," said the master. He placed the grasshopper's hand on his broken nose. "Also."

The grasshopper shipped it Friday, refined it Tuesday, and by Wednesday had bred three bugs with names. He restored the slop.

Later, alone, he sniffed the code, changed one line, fixed the bug, and forgot which line.

## The Book

"Master, how is any of this possible?" asked the grasshopper.

The master led him to the cloud's documentation, bound in iron on a stone pedestal. He pushed it gently; it fell and crushed his foot.

Hopping in a circle, the master pointed at the open page. "Door."

The grasshopper read, and fetched a smaller book.

## Terminology

*A short dictionary, after the manner of a more famous one.*

- **Cloud, n.** A walled garden whose walls are made of paper, and whose captives do not read.
- **Book, the.** A cloud's confession, left out as if by accident in the gift shop.
- **Saddle, the.** That which appears to belong to the horse; in truth, the horse follows it.
- **Shim, n.** A wall on one side, a door on the other.
- **Master, n.** A grasshopper who survived.
- **Blind master, the.** A grasshopper who outlived his eyes. Builds shims by touch; walks into chairs.
- **River, the.** A service that needs no shim. Some grasshoppers, out of kindness, try to help anyway.
- **Helm, the.** Kubernetes. A solution large enough to be mistaken for a problem.
- **Slop, n.** Code that works without being asked twice. The second draft is often worse.
- **Vibe, n.** A direction of motion not yet justified, often correct.
